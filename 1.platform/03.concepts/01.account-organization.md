## One account per person

A fortrabbit account maps to a person in real life. People manage [apps](/1.platform/10.objects/01.app.md), [teams](/1.platform/10.objects/04.team.md), and [payment methods](/1.platform/10.objects/09.payment-method.md).

| Person      | Team                  | App               | Payment method     |
| ----------- | --------------------- | ----------------- | ------------------ |
| human being | a group of developers | website / web app | object owning apps |
| active      | passive               | passive           | passive            |

### Shared accounts

- **No shared logins**: invite colleagues and clients with the [collaboration features](/1.platform/20.collaboration/1.intro.md) instead of sharing a password.
- **No generic addresses**: sign up with `firstname.lastname@company.com`, not `accounts@company.com`.

## When a second account makes sense

### Strict separation

Collaborators see the names of the other apps, teams and payment methods on a profile, without access to them. That helps to ask for access to an app. To keep private projects out of sight of co-workers, use a second account.

### GitHub connection limits

- One GitHub account connects to one fortrabbit account only.
- One fortrabbit account connects to one GitHub account only.

### Not a reason for a second account

- **Another app**: one account holds any number of apps.
- **A new trial**: each new app starts its own [free trial](/1.platform/02.get-started/02.free-trial.md).
- **A client**: use [billing collaboration](/1.platform/20.collaboration/3.billing-collaboration.md).
- **An organization**: use [developer collaboration](/1.platform/20.collaboration/2.developer-collaboration.md) and teams.
