If you book a plan on the 16th day or create an app or environment, the invoice items will be calculated for 15 active usage days. The dollar signs indicate days that would be included in the invoice:

```plain
       April
Mo Tu We Th Fr Sa Su
     -  -  -  -  -
 -  -  -  -  -  -  -
 -  -  -  $  $  $  $
 $  $  $  $  $  $  $
 $  $  $  $
```

The days are added up and invoiced after the month, see [postpaid](/1.platform/12.billing/02.postpaid.md).

## Examples

```yml
# Adding a component mid-month:
Monthly rate: €30
Start date: 15th
Days used: 16
Charge: €16 (€30 ÷ 30 × 16)

# Upgrading a plan:
Old plan: €30/month (€1/day)
New plan: €60/month (€2/day)
Charge: €19 (19 days × €1) + €22 (11 days × €2)
```

## Benefits

Billing by the day makes short experiments cheap:

- Create an environment to test a feature branch for a couple of days
- Test best settings for scaling the production environment
- Scale up hosting resources for temporary events such as media coverage

## The largest plan counts

To find the right PHP plan, someone scales PHP from XS to SM to MD and back on one day, watching the PHP response time. The invoice shows PHP MD for that day: a downgrade takes effect on the next day.

## Monthly prices for reference

Prices are shown per month, as most websites run for months or years.

## 30 days monthly base

Every month counts as 30 days, also February and months with 31 days. A plan booked for the whole month costs its monthly price. For part of a month, the price is 1/30 of the monthly price times the days booked.

## No hourly prices

Hourly prices are hard to compare with a monthly budget, and most projects run for months. A day costs 1/30 of the monthly price, which is a few cents for most plans.
