## About metrics

Metrics are measured on the platform, outside the app. No code, agent, or extension is needed:

- The load balancer counts requests, traffic, and status codes, and times every PHP request.
- The containers report memory, storage, and the OPcache fill level.

The platform takes a sample of each metric at short intervals. The charts group the samples into one bar per hour or day.

:BlockLink{title="See metrics for {{app-name}} / {{app-env-name}}" path="/environments/{{app-env-id}}/metrics"}

## Time frame and resolution

The time frame sets the range and what one bar stands for. Times are UTC.

| Time frame              | One bar                       |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Last 2 days             | Hour                          |
| Last 30, 60, 90 days    | Day                           |
| The two previous months | Day                           |
| Custom range            | Hour up to two days, else day |

The last two days are yesterday and today. The bar of the current hour or day is still filling.

## Reading a chart

The box on the chart shows the figures for the whole time frame:

- Hover over a bar, or click it, to see the values of that hour or day in the box. Click the bar again to clear the selection.
- A max or min figure names the day it happened. Click the day to select that bar.
- A sum carries no label: the bare number under "404 errors" is their count.
- Drag the box aside when it covers bars.

Around the chart:

- Horizontal lines mark thresholds, such as Slow for PHP response time, or the plan sizes of the component, such as the memory of each PHP plan.
- The Scale slider sets the value at the top of the chart. Move it left to enlarge small bars, right to bring back bars that run out of the chart.
- Links below the chart open the logs behind the metric for that time frame, as long as the logs reach back that far.

## Labels per metric

Which values each metric shows in one bar, in the box for the time frame, and as its headline on the summary page. Lines lists the horizontal lines.

What the labels stand for:

| Label        | Counts                     | Storage, memory, OPcache | PHP response time    |
| ------------ | -------------------------- | ------------------------ | -------------------- |
| sum          | All counted in the period  | –                        | –                    |
| max          | Highest bar                | Highest sample           | Slowest request      |
| min          | Lowest bar                 | Lowest sample            | –                    |
| last         | –                          | Latest sample            | –                    |
| avg          | –                          | Average of the samples   | Average request      |
| typical peak | –                          | Median sample            | –                    |
| med          | –                          | –                        | Middle request: half were faster |
| 99ile        | –                          | –                        | 99 of 100 requests were faster   |

## Samples and bars

### Counts

PHP requests, static requests, traffic, the HTTP errors, and OOM kills. A sample holds what happened since the one before: requests answered, bytes sent and received, responses with that status code, processes killed. A bar is the sum of its samples.

In the box, the sum covers the whole time frame. Max and min are the highest and the lowest bar, so max shows the busiest hour or day. Error counts cover PHP and static files alike.

### Storage

Web storage and MySQL storage. A sample holds the size in use at that moment. A bar shows four values from its samples:

- max: the largest size of the hour or day
- last: the size at the end of the hour or day
- avg: the average size
- min: the smallest size

A last well below the max of the same day means the size dropped during that day, for example after a cleanup. In the box, the same four values cover every sample of the time frame.

### Memory

PHP memory and jobs memory. A sample holds the highest memory the containers used since the sample before, so it catches short spikes. A bar shows two values:

- max: the highest sample of the hour or day. Compare it with the memory of the plan, drawn as a line.
- typical peak: the median sample. Half of the samples were lower, half higher.

A typical peak far below the max means the memory stayed low most of the time and spiked now and then.

### PHP OPcache

A sample holds the highest OPcache fill level since the sample before. A bar shows the highest sample of the hour or day. The box shows last, the fill level at the latest sample, and max, the highest bar. Keep it below the Nearly full line.

### PHP response time

The load balancer times every PHP request. It keeps three things: how many requests fell into each duration range, the sum of all durations, and the slowest request. Every value is computed from these:

- max: the slowest single request of the hour or day. It is exact. One stuck request sets it, so it often runs out of the chart.
- avg, med, 99ile: one of the three, picked under "Measure", see [average, median and 99ile](/1.platform/07.settings/31.php-response-time.md).

The figures in the box are computed from all requests of the time frame at once, not from the bars. The fastest request is not recorded. Median and 99ile are [estimates](/1.platform/07.settings/31.php-response-time.md#estimates).

## Metrics, logs, and APM

Metrics show that something changed, and when. [Logs](/1.platform/07.settings/25.logs.md) show the single request behind it. An [APM tool](/4.integrations/04.apm/01.intro.md) traces a request through the code. Start with the metric, narrow the time frame, then read the logs for that window.
