## About metrics

Metrics are measured on the platform, outside the app. No code, agent, or extension is needed. The platform takes samples at short intervals and groups them into bars.

:BlockLink{title="See metrics for {{app-name}} / {{app-env-name}}" path="/environments/{{app-env-id}}/metrics"}

## Time frame and resolution

The time frame sets the range and what one bar stands for. Times are UTC.

| Time frame              | One bar                       |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Last 2 days             | Hour                          |
| Last 30, 60, 90 days    | Day                           |
| The two previous months | Day                           |
| Custom range            | Hour up to two days, else day |

The bar of the current hour or day is still filling.

## Reading a chart

- The box on the chart shows the figures for the whole time frame.
- Hover over a bar, or click it, to see that hour or day. Click again to clear.
- A max or min names its day. Click the day to select that bar.
- A sum carries no label.
- Horizontal lines mark thresholds or plan sizes.
- The Scale slider sets the top of the chart.
- Links below the chart open the logs of that time frame.

## Samples and bars

### Counts

Requests, traffic, errors, and OOM kills. A sample holds what happened since the one before. A bar is the sum of its samples. In the box, max and min are the highest and the lowest bar.

### Storage

Web storage and MySQL storage. A sample holds the size at that moment. The value labeled last is the size at the end of the bar or the time frame.

### Memory

PHP memory and jobs memory. A sample holds the highest memory used since the one before. The value labeled max is the highest sample, typical peak the median sample. Compare max with the memory of the plan.

### PHP OPcache

A sample holds the highest fill level since the one before.

### PHP response time

The load balancer times every PHP request. The value labeled max is the slowest request. The box figures come from all requests of the time frame, not from the bars. The fastest request is not recorded.

## Average, median and 99ile

The "Measure" select picks which of the three PHP response time shows below max. Example for all three: an hour with 1,000 requests. 999 took 100 ms, one was stuck for 60 s.

### Average

All durations added up, divided by the number of requests. The default. In the example: 160 ms. One stuck request lifts the average of the whole hour.

### Median

Half of the requests were faster, half slower. In the example: the 500th request, 100 ms. It shows what a typical request took.

### 99ile

99 of 100 requests were faster. In the example: the 990th request, still 100 ms, as only one request was slow. With 20 stuck requests, the 990th is one of them, and the 99ile jumps to 60 s. A slow kind of request shows up here once it hits more than one in 100 requests.

### Estimates

The average and max are exact. The median and the 99ile are estimates: the load balancer counts requests per duration range, not each duration. The platform finds the range that holds the 500th or 990th request and assumes the requests in it are spread evenly.

## Metrics, logs, and APM

Metrics show that something changed, and when. [Logs](/1.platform/07.settings/25.logs.md) show the single request behind it. An [APM tool](/4.integrations/04.apm/01.intro.md) traces a request through the code. Start with the metric, narrow the time frame, then read the logs for that window.
