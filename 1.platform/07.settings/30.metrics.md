## About metrics

Metrics are measured on the platform, outside the app: the load balancer counts requests and times responses, memory, storage, and OPcache are read from the containers. No code, agent, or extension is needed. Every five minutes the platform stores one sample per metric. The charts group the samples into bars.

:BlockLink{title="See metrics for {{app-name}} / {{app-env-name}}" path="/environments/{{app-env-id}}/metrics"}

The environment overview shows web storage, MySQL storage, and traffic against the booked plan, with traffic counted from the first of the month.

## Time frame and resolution

The time frame sets the range and what one bar stands for. Times are UTC.

| Time frame              | One bar                       |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Last 2 days             | Hour                          |
| Last 30, 60, 90 days    | Day                           |
| The two previous months | Day                           |
| Custom range            | Hour up to two days, else day |

The last two days are yesterday and today. The bar of the current hour or day is still filling.

## Reading a chart

The box on the chart shows the figures for the whole time frame. Hover over a bar, or click it, to see the values of that hour or day instead. Click the bar again to clear the selection. A max or min figure names the day it was recorded on; click the day to select that bar. Drag the box aside when it covers bars.

A sum carries no label. The bare number under "503 errors" is their count. Every other value is labeled max, min, avg, med, 99ile, last, or typical peak. The sections below say how each one is measured.

Horizontal lines mark thresholds or plan sizes. PHP response time has lines at 150 ms (Fast), 350 ms (Good), and 700 ms (Slow). PHP OPcache has one at 90% (Nearly full). Web storage, MySQL storage, PHP memory, and jobs memory draw a line per plan size of their component. Traffic and the counts have no lines.

The Scale slider sets the value at the top of the chart. A bar above it runs out of the chart; move the slider right to bring it back, left to enlarge small bars.

Below the chart, links open the logs behind the metric at the end of its time frame: the access logs for traffic, requests, and 404 errors, the PHP logs for response time, memory, and OOM kills, the jobs logs for the jobs metrics, and both PHP and access logs for 500 and 504 errors. The links show as long as the logs reach back that far.

## What each value measures

What a sample holds, and how a bar is made from the samples, differs per metric family.

### Counts

PHP requests, static requests, traffic, the HTTP errors, and OOM kills are counts. A sample holds what happened in its five minutes: requests answered, bytes sent and received, responses with that status, processes killed. A bar is the sum of its samples. The figures of the time frame are the total of all bars, max for the highest bar, and min for the lowest. Traffic shows total and max, OOM kills only the total. Error counts cover PHP and static files alike.

### Storage

Web storage and MySQL storage are sizes. A sample holds the size in use at that moment. A bar holds four values from its samples: max, last (the size at the end of the hour or day), avg, and min. A last well below the max of the same day means the size dropped during the day. The figures of the time frame use every sample in it: last is the size at the latest sample, max and min the highest and lowest sample, avg their average.

### Memory

PHP memory and jobs memory are peaks. A sample holds the highest memory the container used in its five minutes. A bar shows max, the highest sample, and typical peak, the median sample: half of the five-minute peaks of the hour or day were below it, half above. The max is the value to compare with the memory of the plan. A typical peak far below the max means the memory stayed low in at least half of the five-minute windows. The figures of the time frame are max and typical peak over all samples in it.

### PHP OPcache

A sample holds the highest fill level in its five minutes. A bar holds the highest sample, labeled max. The figures of the time frame are last, the fill level at the latest sample, and max, the highest bar.

### PHP response time

The load balancer times every PHP request. Per minute it records how many requests fell into each duration range, the sum of all durations, and the slowest request. A sample carries those over five minutes. A bar shows max, the slowest request of the hour or day, and the measure picked under "Measure": avg, med, or 99ile. The figures of the time frame are the same two values, computed over every request of the time frame rather than from the bars. The max is exact. The fastest request is not shown.

## Average, median and 99ile

The three measures describe the same requests from different angles. The example for all three: 1,000 PHP requests in one hour.

### Average

The durations of all requests added up, divided by their number. It is exact and the default measure. Suppose 999 requests took 100 ms and one was stuck for 60 s: 999 × 100 ms + 60,000 ms = 159,900 ms, divided by 1,000 gives 160 ms. One stuck request lifts the average from 100 ms to 160 ms, although 999 requests took 100 ms. Multiplied by the number of requests, the average gives the PHP time the hour spent, which the processes of the PHP component have to cover.

### Median

Half of the requests were faster than the median, half slower. In the example the 500th fastest request sets it, and that one took 100 ms, with or without the stuck request. The median says what a typical request took. It depends on how many requests were slow, and not on how slow they were.

### 99ile

The 99th percentile: 99 of 100 requests were faster, one in 100 slower. In the example the 990th fastest request sets it, and the ten slowest requests of the hour took longer. With one stuck request the 99ile stays at 100 ms, as the 990th request is a fast one. Were 20 requests stuck instead, the 990th would be one of them, and the 99ile would jump to 60 s. A slow kind of request shows up in the 99ile once more than one percent of the requests are that slow. Where the average says what the hour cost in PHP time, the 99ile says what the slowest one percent of requests waited.

### How the median and the 99ile are estimated

The load balancer does not keep the duration of every request. It counts requests per duration range, each range bounded by an upper limit. For the 99ile of 1,000 requests, the platform looks for the 990th: it walks the ranges, adding up their counts, until the sum reaches 990. Suppose 985 requests took up to 500 ms and 995 took up to 1 s. The 990th lies in the range from 500 ms to 1 s. The estimate assumes the ten requests in that range are spread evenly: 500 ms + 500 ms × (990 − 985) / (995 − 985) = 750 ms. The true 990th request took somewhere between 500 ms and 1 s. The estimate never exceeds the max. The median is estimated the same way at the 500th request.

## Summary

The metrics tab opens on the summary, one teaser per metric with its headline figure and small bars. The headline is the total for counts, last for storage and OPcache, max for memory, and avg for response time. Hover over a column to see that hour or day in every teaser at once; the field next to the time frame shows its time. Click a teaser to open the chart.

## Metrics, logs, and APM

Metrics show that something changed, and when. [Logs](/1.platform/07.settings/25.logs.md) show the single request behind it. An [APM tool](/4.integrations/04.apm/01.intro.md) traces a request through the code. Start with the metric, narrow the time frame, then read the logs for that window.
