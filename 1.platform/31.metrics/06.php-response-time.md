## About PHP response time

The time is measured at the load balancer in front of PHP, for each request that PHP handles. Static files are not included. Each bar shows the lowest value, the 99th percentile and the highest value of its hour or day. The summary adds the median and the average of the whole time frame. Lines at 100 ms, 300 ms and 500 ms mark fast, average and slow.

:BlockLink{title="See PHP response time for {{app-env-name}}" path="/environments/{{app-env-id}}/metrics/php-response-time"}

## What to look for

The 99th percentile is the figure to watch. Single high maximums are normal: a cold cache, a large export, a cron URL. A 99th percentile that rises over days points to growing data or slower code. Response times that rise together with [PHP requests](/1.platform/31.metrics/07.php-requests.md) mean PHP runs short of processes.

## What to do

Find the slow URLs first: an [APM tool](/4.integrations/04.apm/01.intro.md) or a profiler shows where the time goes. Common causes are slow database queries, calls to external APIs and missing caching, see [backend performance](/3.dev/02.performance/05.backend.md). Scale the [PHP component](/1.platform/09.components/01.php.md#scaling) when the code is fine and the load is real. Requests that take too long end as [504 errors](/1.platform/31.metrics/16.error-504.md).
