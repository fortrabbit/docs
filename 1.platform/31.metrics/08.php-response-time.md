## About PHP response time

The time is measured at the load balancer in front of PHP, for every request PHP handles. Static files are not included. Each bar shows the lowest value, the mean measure, and the highest value of its hour or day. The mean measure is the 99th percentile by default; switch it to the median or the average in the options. Lines at 100 ms, 300 ms, and 500 ms mark fast, average, and slow.

:BlockLink{title="See PHP response time for {{app-env-name}}" path="/environments/{{app-env-id}}/metrics/php-response-time"}

## What to look for

Watch the 99th percentile. Single high maximums are normal: a cold cache, a large export, a cron URL. A 99th percentile that rises over days points to growing data or slower code. Response times that rise together with [PHP requests](/1.platform/31.metrics/09.php-requests.md) mean PHP runs short of processes.

## What to do

Find the slow URLs first: the table of [PHP requests](/1.platform/31.metrics/09.php-requests.md) shows the average duration per URL, an [APM tool](/4.integrations/04.apm/01.intro.md) shows where the time goes. Common causes are slow database queries, calls to external APIs, and missing caching, see [backend performance](/3.dev/02.performance/05.backend.md). Scale the [PHP component](/1.platform/09.components/01.php.md#scaling) when the code is fast and the load is real. Requests that take too long end as [504 errors](/1.platform/31.metrics/19.error-504.md).
