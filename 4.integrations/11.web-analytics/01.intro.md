Web analytics show where visitors come from, which pages they read and what they click.

## Privacy-focused analytics

Services such as [Fathom](/4.integrations/11.web-analytics/02.fathom.md), [Plausible](/4.integrations/11.web-analytics/03.plausible.md) and [Rybbit](/4.integrations/11.web-analytics/05.rybbit.md) count visits without cookies and without storing personal data. Whether a site still needs a consent banner depends on the law of each country; check with the service and a lawyer.

## Integration with fortrabbit

Each service provides a JavaScript snippet for the page templates. fortrabbit needs no setup for it.

## Relation to fortrabbit metrics

The [metrics](/1.platform/26.metrics/01.intro.md) of fortrabbit count on the server: requests, traffic and errors, including bots and requests the browser script never sees. Web analytics count in the browser: visitors, sessions and clicks, without most bots. The numbers differ and do not compare one to one. The [access logs](/1.platform/07.settings/25.logs.md#access-logs) show every single request.

## Blocked scripts

Ad blockers and browser privacy features block many tracking scripts, of privacy-focused services too. Browser analytics therefore miss part of the visitors. Some services offer a proxy through the own domain to reduce that.

## APM tools with analytics features

Some [APM tools](/4.integrations/04.apm/01.intro.md) include analytics. PostHog, for example, combines web analytics, error tracking and product analytics, at more cost and complexity than an analytics service alone.
